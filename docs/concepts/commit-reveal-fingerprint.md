# Commit–reveal fingerprint (Level 1.5)

The ZK proof guarantees the bug is real. But once the hunter is paid, what stops them from sending the creator a **fake** exploit ("here's the trick" — but it's garbage)? Level 1.5 makes the reveal **unfakeable**, cheaply.

## The smoothie analogy

Think of a magic blender:

* You put in secret ingredients (`a`, `b`, and a random `salt`) → out comes a **uniquely-coloured smoothie**.
* The same ingredients always give the same colour.
* But from the smoothie, you **cannot reverse-engineer** the ingredients.

That smoothie colour is a **hash** (SHA-256). It's a fingerprint.

## What the guest does

When the guest checks the exploit, it also computes:

```
fingerprint = sha256( a (16 bytes LE) ‖ b (16 bytes LE) ‖ salt (32 bytes) )
```

and **commits the fingerprint to the journal** (which is public and ends up bound into the proof on-chain). The secret `a`, `b`, `salt` are **not** committed — the fingerprint can't be reversed, so they stay secret.

> Hashing is extremely cheap inside the zkVM. Adding the fingerprint did **not** measurably increase proving time or memory in our tests — unlike full encryption-in-zkVM, which is heavy. See [future work](../reference/future-work.md) for why we didn't go further (yet).

## Why the salt matters

The factoring input space is small (only a few hundred factor pairs of 1,000,000). If we hashed only `a` and `b`, anyone reading the public journal could brute-force all pairs and find which matches — leaking the secret. The **random 32-byte salt** (generated by the host, kept secret, included only in the reveal) makes the fingerprint impossible to brute-force.

## How the creator verifies the reveal

1. The hunter sends the creator the encrypted `reveal.json` = `{a, b, salt}`.
2. The creator decrypts it with their private key.
3. The Veil app recomputes `sha256(a ‖ b ‖ salt)` **in the browser** and compares it to the fingerprint stored on-chain (extracted from the winning proof's journal).

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ✅ match | This is the **real** exploit that won the bounty. The hunter cannot have faked it. |
| ❌ mismatch | Fake or wrong reveal — caught instantly. |

The fingerprint is locked at the moment the proof is created, so the hunter cannot change their story afterward.

## Cross-layer consistency

The same SHA-256 is computed in three places and they all match (we verified this):

* the **guest** (RISC Zero `sha256`),
* the **host** (Rust `sha2` crate),
* the **frontend** (browser Web Crypto `SHA-256`).

This guarantees the creator's in-browser check agrees with what's bound in the proof.

## What this layer does and doesn't solve

* ✅ Solves **lying**: the hunter cannot send a fake exploit.
* ❌ Does **not** solve **ghosting**: a hunter could get paid and simply never reveal anything.

Plugging the ghosting hole is the job of the next layer → [Stake & reveal](stake-and-reveal.md).
